Why this exists

Most of the world knows one biriyani. India knows hundreds.

Biriyani is a love letter to a dish that never stayed still. It moved with empires, trains, ports, wedding cooks, military hotels, neighborhood cafes, and migrant families. Every city bent it to local rice, local spice, local appetite.

This archive exists so every regional memory gets equal space: the potato in Kolkata, the leaf cup in Bengaluru, the short-grain elegance of Thalassery, the street-side clarity of Moradabad, the Arcot corridor in Tamil Nadu, the community tables of Bohri and Memoni cooks.

If you grew up with one version, this is an invitation to meet the rest.
